> **In 30 Seconds**
> - **Definition:** Treatment-Resistant Depression (TRD) generally refers to major depression that has not responded adequately to at least two antidepressant trials of sufficient dose and duration.
> - **Initial Step:** Before switching treatments, the diagnosis, dose, duration, adherence, and comorbid medical/psychiatric conditions must be rigorously reviewed.
> - **Core Options:** Key options include switching or augmenting medications, psychotherapy, [rTMS](/en/rtms), esketamine or IV ketamine, and electroconvulsive therapy (ECT).
> - **Personalization:** No single strategy works for everyone. The treatment sequence depends on medical history, symptom severity, and urgency of response.

---

## What is Treatment-Resistant Depression and How is it Defined?

Treatment-Resistant Depression (TRD) is generally defined as [major depressive disorder](/en/diataraxes/katathlipsi) that fails to show satisfactory improvement following at least two adequate trials of antidepressants. There is no single universal definition; CANMAT 2023 guidelines also employ the broader term "difficult-to-treat depression" [[1]](#ref-1).

TRD is **not** an incurable condition. It simply means that first-line treatments have proven insufficient and a more structured, multidimensional strategy is needed. In the benchmark STAR*D trial, the cumulative probability of achieving remission decreased with each subsequent treatment step: 36.8%, 30.6%, 13.7%, and 13.0% across the four sequential steps [[2]](#ref-2).

---

## What Does "Treatment Failure" Mean for the Patient?

Treatment failure means that a specific drug or intervention did not achieve the required clinical endpoint. **It does not mean that the patient "failed" or that there are no remaining solutions.** In clinical practice, we distinguish:
- **Non-response:** Symptoms remain unchanged.
- **Partial response:** There is noticeable improvement, but residual symptoms continue to impair daily functioning.
- **Discontinuation due to adverse effects:** The medication was intolerable. Strictly speaking, intolerance does not equal biological resistance.

This precise distinction directs the next therapeutic step.

---

## What Should I Do When Antidepressants Stop Working?

**Never discontinue medication abruptly on your own.** Sudden cessation can trigger withdrawal syndromes or acute relapses. Seek an [in-depth psychiatric evaluation and second opinion](/en/ypiresies/psychiatriki-parakolouthisi).

Prior to altering treatment, the psychiatrist systematically re-evaluates:
1. **The diagnosis:** [Bipolar disorder](/en/diataraxes/dipoliki-diatarachi), comorbid [anxiety disorders](/en/diataraxes/agxodeis-diataraxes), or organic conditions (e.g. thyroid dysregulation) can mimic or perpetuate resistant depression.
2. **The dosage and duration** of each prior medication trial.
3. **Treatment adherence.**
4. **Perpetuating lifestyle factors:** [sleep architecture and insomnia](/en/diataraxes/diataraxes-ypnou), alcohol or substance use, and chronic life stressors.
5. **[Pharmacogenomics (PGx)](/en/ypiresies/pgx), when clinically indicated:** DNA testing reveals individual metabolic capacity for psychiatric medications, guiding molecule selection and dosage optimization without prolonged trial-and-error [[3]](#ref-3).

## Core Therapeutic Options for TRD

- **Switching antidepressants** to an alternate pharmacological class.
- **Augmentation therapy** with secondary agents (e.g. aripiprazole or lithium) [[1]](#ref-1).
- **[Psychotherapy](/en/ypiresies/psychotherapeia)** (e.g. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy / CBT), alone or combined with pharmacology.
- **[Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (rTMS)](/en/rtms/endixeis/trd)**.
- **Esketamine or IV ketamine**.
- **Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT)**.

---

## Which Treatment is Recommended First?

There is no "one-size-fits-all" algorithm. Sequencing depends on:
- Prior medications tested and clinical outcomes,
- Severity and acute risk (e.g. suicidal ideation, functional collapse),
- Clinical urgency for rapid symptom relief,
- Patient preferences, tolerability history, and practical accessibility.

According to CANMAT guidelines, aripiprazole augmentation is first-line after two antidepressant failures, while lithium and ketamine/esketamine serve as second-line pharmacological options [[1]](#ref-1). In neurostimulation, [rTMS](/en/rtms) is recognized as first-line for patients who have failed at least one antidepressant trial, whereas ECT is classified as second-line (or primary in life-threatening emergencies) [[4]](#ref-4).

## What is the Evidence for rTMS in Treatment-Resistant Depression?

[rTMS](/en/rtms/core/ti-einai) is an FDA-cleared, non-invasive therapeutic technology for depression and anxiety when standard pharmacotherapy has proven inadequate.

Magnetic pulses stimulate underactive cortical networks (specifically the [left DLPFC](/en/rtms/pos-leitourgei/dlpfc)) involved in mood regulation. It requires no anesthesia or hospitalization; patients remain fully awake and resume daily activities immediately after each session.

**Pivotal Trial:** The landmark multicenter trial by O’Reardon et al. (2007) evaluated 301 unmedicated patients with major depression who had failed previous treatments, randomized to active rTMS (155) or sham stimulation (146) [[5]](#ref-5). Active rTMS demonstrated clear superiority over sham at weeks 4 and 6 on MADRS and HDRS scales, leading to the first FDA clearance for TRD in 2008 [[5]](#ref-5).

**Key Findings:**
- In randomized controlled meta-analyses, unilateral rTMS achieved remission rates of 16.0% and response rates of 25.1% versus 5.7% and 11.0% for sham [[6]](#ref-6).
- **rTMS vs. iTBS:** Intermittent Theta Burst Stimulation (iTBS) reduces session time to just 3 minutes. In the *Lancet* THREE-D trial, response and remission rates were 49% and 32% for iTBS versus 47% and 27% for standard 10 Hz rTMS, confirming non-inferiority [[7]](#ref-7).
- **Accelerated Protocols (SNT / SAINT):** High-dose iTBS with fMRI-guided targeting delivered over 5 days. Cole et al. (2022) reported a 79% remission rate within 4 weeks, receiving FDA clearance in September 2022 [[8]](#ref-8).
- Response is systematically measured using standardized depression scales (such as [PHQ-9](/psychometrika/phq9)).
- The most common side effect is mild, transient scalp discomfort or headache [[7]](#ref-7).

## Comparing Ketamine and Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT)

The most direct comparison is the **ELEKT-D trial (*NEJM* 2023)**, a non-inferiority trial across five US centers involving 365 treated patients. At 3 weeks, 55.4% in the IV ketamine group achieved a clinical response compared to 41.2% with ECT (difference of 14.2 percentage points), demonstrating non-inferiority [[9]](#ref-9). ECT was associated with musculoskeletal side effects and transient memory impairment, while ketamine was associated with temporary dissociation [[9]](#ref-9).

These results apply to non-psychotic TRD and do not automatically generalize to intranasal esketamine, which has its own dosing and regulatory framework.

## Weighing Risks and Benefits: Esketamine vs. IV Ketamine

| Parameter | Intranasal Esketamine (Spravato) | Intravenous Ketamine |
|---|---|---|
| **Indication** | Treatment-resistant depression, combined with an oral antidepressant | Treatment-resistant depression, based on clinical judgment |
| **Evidence** | **ESCAPE-TRD (NEJM 2023):** 27.1% remission at week 8 vs. 17.6% with quetiapine XR [[10]](#ref-10) | **ELEKT-D (NEJM 2023):** 55.4% response vs. 41.2% with ECT [[9]](#ref-9) |
| **Administration** | Specialized clinical setting under direct observation (initial twice-weekly) | Controlled medical / hospital setting |
| **Adverse Effects** | Dizziness, dissociation, transient blood pressure elevation | Dissociation, hemodynamic fluctuations [[9]](#ref-9) |
| **Maintenance** | Ongoing tapered maintenance sessions typically required | Highly variable; requires individualized planning |
| **Safety** | Pre-screening for cardiovascular risk, psychosis, and substance use history | Identical precautions |

## Practical Comparison: rTMS vs. ECT

| Feature | rTMS (Magnetic Stimulation) | ECT (Electroconvulsive Therapy) |
|---|---|---|
| **Anesthesia** | None | Required (general anesthesia & muscle relaxants) |
| **Hospitalization** | Outpatient clinic sessions | Often requires hospital admission |
| **Recovery** | Immediate return to daily activities | Requires post-anesthesia recovery monitoring |
| **Primary Indication** | TRD without immediate life danger, seeking non-invasive therapy [[4]](#ref-4) | Severe depression, psychosis, catatonia, immediate acute crisis [[4]](#ref-4), [[11]](#ref-11) |

## Investigational Therapies

- **Psilocybin:** COMP360 synthetic psilocybin (Compass Pathways) demonstrated significant MADRS reductions at week 6 in Phase 3 trials (COMP005 and COMP006). FDA New Drug Application was submitted in November 2026 [[12]](#ref-12). It remains an investigational, non-cleared therapy.
- **ONE-D Protocol:** Delivering 20 iTBS sessions in a single day combined with off-label pharmacotherapy. Early reports are exploratory [[13]](#ref-13); the Clinical TMS Society issued an advisory in December 2025 emphasizing that existing evidence remains insufficient [[14]](#ref-14).
